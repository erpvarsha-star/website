## The short answer

Most buyers need either Level 3 or Level 5 from a forging supplier. Level 3 is the common default for new parts. Level 5 is for the most critical parts, or for a new supplier, when you want to see the evidence at the plant itself.

Levels 1 and 2 are lighter. Level 4 is whatever you define. Choose the level by the risk of the part, not by habit.

## What PPAP is

PPAP stands for Production Part Approval Process. It comes from the automotive quality world and sits alongside APQP, the planning method that comes before it.

The idea is simple. Before a supplier ships series parts, it proves that its process can make the part to your drawing, again and again. The proof goes to you as a submission package. You review it and approve, or reject, before volume starts.

The cover sheet of every submission is the Part Submission Warrant, usually called the PSW. It is the supplier's signed statement that the parts meet the drawing and the process is ready.

## What goes into a PPAP package

A full package can hold up to 18 elements. For a forged and machined part, the ones buyers look at most are these:

- **Design records.** The drawing and any engineering change notes.
- **Process flow diagram.** Every step from raw material to dispatch.
- **Process FMEA and control plan.** What can go wrong at each step, and how the supplier prevents or detects it.
- **Dimensional results.** Measured results for the parts submitted, against every drawing dimension.
- **Material and performance test results.** Chemistry, hardness and any other test the drawing calls for.
- **Measurement system analysis.** Proof that the gauges and machines used to measure can be trusted.
- **Capability studies.** Statistical evidence that key characteristics stay within limits over time.
- **Qualified laboratory documentation.** Evidence that the testing lab is competent for the tests it ran.
- **Sample parts and the master sample.** Physical parts to compare against.
- **The PSW.** The signed summary of the whole package.

## The five PPAP levels

The levels decide how much you receive, and where you receive it.

| Level | What the supplier submits | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The PSW only, plus an appearance report if one applies | Low-risk, simple or catalogue parts |
| 2 | The PSW with sample parts and limited supporting data | Moderate risk, or a repeat part with small changes |
| 3 | The PSW with sample parts and complete supporting data | The usual default for new automotive parts |
| 4 | The PSW and whatever other items you define | Special cases where you name the exact contents |
| 5 | The PSW, sample parts and complete data, reviewed at the supplier's plant | Critical parts, new suppliers, or high-risk changes |

The wording above is the general industry pattern. Your own customer-specific requirements can change what each level means, so always read them.

## How to choose a level

Ask three questions.

**How dangerous is a failure?** For safety-critical parts such as steering and suspension components, ask for Level 3 at least. Consider Level 5 for a new design or a new supplier.

**How new is the supplier to you?** A first order from a new source carries more risk than a repeat order from a proven one. Higher levels give you more evidence when trust is not yet built.

**What has changed?** A new die, a new material source, a new heat treatment route or a new plant should trigger a fresh submission. Say in the purchase terms which changes need your approval first.

If you are unsure, state Level 3 in the RFQ and ask the supplier to confirm it can also support Level 5 if you want to audit.

## What to check in a forging supplier's submission

The package can look complete and still be weak. Read it with these checks.

- **Does the process flow match the real plant?** Forging, heat treatment, machining and inspection steps should all appear, and any outsourced step should be named.
- **Do the dimensional results cover every feature?** Not only the easy ones.
- **Is the material traceable?** The certificate should link to the steel heat used for the submitted parts.
- **Are the capability studies built on real production data?** Ask how many parts and over what period.
- **Are the control plan and FMEA specific to this part?** Copied generic documents are a warning sign.
- **Who approved it?** The PSW should be signed by a responsible person at the supplier.

## What a Level 5 review looks like

At Level 5 you or your representative reviews the full evidence at the supplier's site. You can see the parts, the gauges, the records and the process together.

It is the strongest form of approval, and it is also the most work for both sides. Use it where the risk justifies it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the default PPAP level?**
Many automotive customers treat Level 3 as the default. Your own customer-specific requirements decide this, so check them.

**Is Level 5 better than Level 3?**
It is more thorough, because the evidence is reviewed at the plant. It is not always needed. Use it for critical parts or new suppliers.

**When do I need a new PPAP submission?**
After changes such as a new die, new material source or new process route. Agree the list of triggers with the supplier before the first order.

**Can a supplier support all five levels?**
Yes, if it has the systems and records. Varsha Forgings supports Levels 1 to 5.

**Where does PPAP fit in the quoting process?**
After the quotation. At Varsha Forgings the sequence is customer drawing, feasibility review, quotation, then PPAP.
