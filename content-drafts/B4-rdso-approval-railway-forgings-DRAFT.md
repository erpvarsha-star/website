## The short answer

RDSO approval is a gate. In India, railway procurement normally allows only approved sources for the items covered. If a supplier says it is RDSO approved, your job is to confirm three things: that the approval is current, that it covers the exact item you want, and that the plant behind it can repeat the quality.

This guide is a checklist for doing that. It is general. Rules and processes change, so always confirm the current position with the railway authority or the purchaser.

## What RDSO is, in one paragraph

RDSO is the Research Designs and Standards Organisation, based in Lucknow. It is the technical body that supports Indian Railways with specifications, drawings and the approval of manufacturers. When a railway part has an RDSO drawing or specification, the approved vendor list for that item decides who may supply it.

## Who needs to check an RDSO claim

- **Indian railway buyers and contractors.** Tender rules often require an approved source.
- **Rolling stock builders and their sub-suppliers.** Tier-one firms often pass the approval requirement down the chain.
- **Overseas buyers who build to Indian railway drawings.** If your drawing carries an Indian railway specification, ask which approvals your customer expects.

If your railway customer does not require RDSO approval at all, the checklist below still helps you judge how mature a supplier's quality system is.

## The buyer's checklist

### 1. Ask which item and drawing the approval covers

Approval is normally tied to named items. A firm can be approved for one part and not for another. Ask for the item name, the drawing or specification number and the approval's current status.

### 2. Ask for the document, not a statement

A logo on a website is not evidence. Ask for the approval letter or the vendor list entry. Check the supplier's legal name matches the name on the document.

### 3. Check that the plant on the document is the plant that will make your part

Approval is given to a manufacturing location. If a trader or a second plant would make your order, the approval may not apply. Ask where each process step happens, and which steps are outsourced.

### 4. Check the validity and any conditions

Approvals are reviewed from time to time. Ask for the validity position and any conditions attached, such as limits on quantity or item type.

### 5. Look at traceability

For railway parts you should be able to link each finished forging to its steel heat, its heat treatment batch and its inspection record. Ask to see a sample record set, with identifiers blanked if needed.

### 6. Review the testing evidence

Ask which tests run on each batch and which on every part. Look for chemistry, hardness, grain structure and crack testing. Ask who witnesses or approves the results.

### 7. Check the wider quality system

Approval from a railway body does not replace a general quality system. Ask for the current certificate to IATF 16949 or ISO 9001, and look at how corrective actions are handled.

### 8. Visit, or take a virtual tour

Nothing replaces seeing the plant. If you cannot travel, ask for a virtual tour of the forging, heat treatment and inspection areas.

## Red flags

- The supplier cannot name the items covered.
- The approval document is in a different company name.
- The supplier refuses an audit or a plant visit.
- Material certificates do not show the steel heat.
- The supplier quotes performance figures for the part that the drawing does not request and the supplier cannot back with records.

## Common mistakes buyers make

**Treating approval as quality in general.** It confirms eligibility for named items. It does not remove the need for your own incoming checks.

**Assuming approval transfers.** Approval does not pass automatically to a new plant or a new firm.

**Skipping the drawing check.** Many problems come from a drawing revision that does not match the approved item. Confirm the revision before you order.

**Ignoring other approvals.** Some railway buyers also ask for approvals from other bodies. Confirm what your customer expects, and do not assume that one approval stands in for another.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is RDSO approval the same as a quality certificate?**
No. It is an approval to supply named railway items. A quality certificate such as IATF 16949 covers the management system. Good suppliers hold both.

**Does approval cover every railway part a supplier makes?**
Not automatically. Check the items and drawings covered.

**Can I source railway forgings from India for export?**
Yes. Agree the drawing, specification, testing and documents in the RFQ. Be clear about which approvals your own customer requires.

**How do I verify a supplier is on the list?**
Ask for the approval document and check it with the purchaser or the issuing body.

**What should I send with an enquiry?**
The drawing, the RDSO specification number if any, the material grade, the annual volume and the documents you need.
