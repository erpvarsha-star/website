## The short answer

A supplier audit asks one question: can this plant make my part right, every time, and prove it? A certificate on the wall does not answer that. Records, equipment and people do.

This checklist has eight areas. Use it for a first visit, a remote audit by video, or a review before you award business. Take notes, ask to see records, and follow one real part through the plant from steel to dispatch.

## Before you go: prepare

- **Pick a sample part.** Choose a current part similar to yours. Ask to trace it end to end.
- **Request documents in advance.** Certificates, a plant layout, the control plan and a list of outsourced processes.
- **Set the scope.** Decide whether you audit the system, the process for your part, or both.
- **Bring a short list of must-haves.** Material grades, tolerances, testing and the PPAP level you need.

## 1. Quality system and certificates

**What to check.** Current certificates for the management system, such as IATF 16949 and ISO 9001. Read the scope, which should include forging. Look at how the plant handles complaints, corrective actions and internal audits.

**Good looks like.** Recent audit records, closed corrective actions and a clear owner for quality.

**Red flag.** Certificates in another company name or for another site.

## 2. Raw material

**What to check.** Where the steel comes from, how incoming bars are inspected and how material is stored and identified.

**Good looks like.** Steel from established mills, with a certificate per heat, a chemistry check on arrival and clear marking in the yard.

**Red flag.** Mixed, unmarked bars, or no check on incoming chemistry.

## 3. Forging process and tooling

**What to check.** The heating method and temperature control, the press or hammer used for your part, and how dies are made, maintained and tracked.

**Good looks like.** Controlled heating, a die shop on site or a clear die-life record, and a defined process route for each part.

**Red flag.** No die records, or a process that depends on one operator's judgement.

## 4. Heat treatment

**What to check.** The furnace types, how temperature is controlled and recorded, and how load patterns are set. Ask which heat treatments the plant does itself and which it outsources.

**Good looks like.** Recorded cycles for each batch and hardness testing after treatment.

**Red flag.** Heat treatment outsourced with no incoming check on results.

## 5. Machining and finishing

**What to check.** Which machining the supplier does itself and which it passes to vendors. If vendors are used, ask how they are approved and audited.

**Good looks like.** Clear control of outsourced steps, with the supplier taking responsibility for the final result.

**Red flag.** Vague answers about who makes what.

## 6. Testing and measurement

**What to check.** The laboratory and the inspection room. Chemistry, hardness, grain structure and crack testing. Calibration records for gauges and instruments.

**Good looks like.** In-house chemistry analysis, hardness testing on every batch, crack testing on safety-critical parts, and gauges within their calibration dates.

**Red flag.** Out-of-date calibration stickers, or tests done only at an outside lab with long delays.

## 7. Traceability and documents

**What to check.** Pick a finished part and ask the plant to trace it back to its steel heat, its forging batch, its heat treatment record and its inspection record. Time how long it takes.

**Good looks like.** The trail is found quickly, and the identifiers on the part match the paperwork.

**Red flag.** Gaps in the trail, or documents written after the event.

## 8. People, planning and delivery

**What to check.** Training records, shift planning, spare capacity and how the plant handles urgent orders. Ask about export packing, shipping documents and communication with overseas customers.

**Good looks like.** Trained operators, a planned maintenance schedule and a named contact for your account.

**Red flag.** No planning data, or one person who knows everything.

## Audit by video: a short guide

If you cannot travel, ask for a live video walk through the plant. Ask the guide to follow your sample part. Request close views of the gauges, the furnace chart, the marked parts and the records. Agree the schedule in advance, and ask for the same documents you would see on a visit.

## After the audit

Write down findings under three headings: must fix before order, should fix soon, and good practice. Agree dates with the supplier. Check the fixes before series supply starts. Keep the audit record with your supplier file for the next review.

## Where Varsha Forgings meets this checklist

Varsha Forgings Private Limited makes hot closed-die forgings in carbon and alloy steels, from 1 kg to 25 kg, at its plant in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad), India. The company has operated since 1989.

**Quality system.** IATF 16949, ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001:2015 and ISO 45001:2018, with PPAP submission at Levels 1 to 5, APQP and IMDS. RDSO approved for Indian Railways.

**Raw material.** Steel comes from tier-one mills. A vacuum emission spectrometer checks chemistry.

**Process and tooling.** Induction billet heating, presses from 800 T to 2,500 T and friction drop hammers from 1 to 3 MT. The die shop is in-house, and dies are made with Creo, SimuFact forming simulation and Dell Cam programming.

**Heat treatment.** A continuous tube furnace, a batch-type furnace, an electrical tempering furnace and an isothermal annealing furnace. Routes include normalizing, double quench and temper, stress relieving, vacuum carburizing and induction hardening.

**Machining and finishing.** Machining and grinding are done in-house. A dedicated base of vendors extends capacity, and some processes, such as rubber bonding, are done through partners under Varsha's quality control.

**Testing.** Brinell, Rockwell and micro-Vickers hardness, ASTM E112 grain size, a microscope with image analyser, ultrasonic testing, and 100% magnetic particle inspection on safety-critical parts. The standards room has a Faro Arm CMM, a CMM, a profile projector and a contour tester. Statistical process control runs on key characteristics at Cpk of 1.67 or better.

**Traceability.** Each raw-material heat gets a batch code. The code is engraved on the die or marked on the part surface. Reports carry the heat number, and the material test report carries the heat number, traceability code and invoice number. Certificates follow EN 10204 type 3.1, with 3.2 on request.

**Visits.** Customers are welcome to visit the plants after an RFQ, and virtual plant tours are available.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a supplier audit take?**
Plan a full day for a first audit of a single plant. A focused review of one part can be shorter.

**Can I audit a forging supplier remotely?**
Yes, by live video, with documents shared in advance. An on-site visit is still best for a new supplier.

**What is the most useful single test?**
Trace one finished part back to its steel heat. It tests the records, the marking and the discipline of the plant together.

**Should I audit the vendors too?**
Ask how the supplier audits them. For critical processes, consider joining one of those audits.

**Can I visit Varsha Forgings?**
Yes. Customers are welcome to visit after an RFQ, and virtual plant tours are available.
