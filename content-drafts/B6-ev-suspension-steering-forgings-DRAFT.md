## The short answer

An electric vehicle still needs the same kinds of suspension and steering parts as any other car or bus. Arms, links, ball joints, tie rods and knuckles do the same job. What changes is the duty they carry.

Most of the change comes from the battery. It adds mass, and it sits low in the vehicle. That shifts the loads that the chassis parts must handle. Design teams respond with revised geometry, new grades or new validation plans. For buyers, this means more questions about fatigue life, traceability and process control.

This post covers forged suspension and steering parts only. It does not cover motors, gearboxes or other powertrain parts.

## Why EV chassis loads are different

A battery pack is heavy. Many electric vehicles weigh more than a comparable combustion model. Heavier vehicles load the suspension harder over every bump, corner and braking event.

Three other effects matter in general terms:

- **Torque delivery.** Electric drive can deliver torque very quickly. Suspension links and mounts can see sharper load changes at launch.
- **Regenerative braking.** Slowing the vehicle through the drive system changes how braking forces travel through the chassis.
- **Mass distribution.** A low, central battery changes how load moves between axles in a corner.

The exact effect depends on the vehicle. Each OEM defines its own load cases. Treat these points as the reason to ask better questions, not as fixed numbers.

## Why forging stays the right process

Suspension and steering parts are safety-critical. A failed link or arm affects control of the vehicle. That does not change with electrification.

Hot closed-die forging shapes steel so that the grain follows the outline of the part. That gives good fatigue resistance on parts that see cyclic and shock loads. Heavier vehicles and sharper load changes make that property more valuable, not less.

If you want the full process comparison, our guide on forging vs casting vs machining covers it. For tie rods in particular, see our tie rod end forgings page.

## What changes in the design brief

When an EV programme moves to a supplier, the drawing often arrives with a few new demands.

**Weight pressure.** Every kilogram of vehicle mass costs range. Engineers look hard at each part and ask whether it can be lighter without losing strength. A near-net forging with a tuned shape can help, because material sits where the stress is.

**Strength and grade selection.** A stronger or tougher steel may be asked for on heavily loaded links. The grade, the heat treatment and the hardness window need agreement early. Do not assume the grade used on a combustion model carries over.

**Noise, vibration and harshness.** Electric vehicles are quiet, so road and chassis noise is easier to hear. Bushes, mounts and joint fits matter more. Surface finish and dimensional consistency on forged and machined features help the joint behave the same way on every vehicle.

**Corrosion protection.** Underbody parts face water, salt and grit. Ask which coating or surface treatment the drawing needs, and confirm who applies it.

## What to ask a forging supplier

Use these questions when you assess a supplier for EV chassis work.

1. **Which parts have you made for electric vehicles?** Ask for the part family, not only a claim of experience.
2. **How do you control dimensions?** Ask for capability data on the key characteristics, not only final inspection.
3. **How do you find hidden cracks?** Ask which parts get 100% crack testing, and by what method.
4. **How is each part traced?** You should be able to link a finished part to its steel heat.
5. **How do you handle design changes?** EV programmes change fast. Ask how revisions are managed and approved.
6. **What validation support do you offer?** Ask about sample parts, PPAP level and test reports.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do electric vehicles need different suspension parts?**
They need the same types of parts. The loads and design targets can differ, so drawings, grades and validation plans may change.

**Are forged parts still the right choice for EV steering?**
For safety-critical steel parts under cyclic load, forging remains a strong choice. The grain follows the part shape and the metal is dense.

**Does Varsha Forgings make EV motor or gearbox parts?**
No. For electric vehicles, Varsha Forgings supplies suspension and steering parts.
