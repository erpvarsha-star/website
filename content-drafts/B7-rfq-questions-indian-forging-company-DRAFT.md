## The short answer

A forging RFQ fails most often because it is incomplete. The supplier has to guess, and a guess becomes a wide quote, a long delay or a surprise later.

Spend an hour on five questions before you send the request. Your answers will shape the quote, and they will show how well the supplier responds. Good suppliers ask sharp questions back.

## Question 1: Is the drawing ready to quote?

Start with the drawing. A supplier cannot judge feasibility without it.

Send a 2D drawing, a 3D model, or both. Show the finished part and mark the critical dimensions. If you want a forged blank that you machine yourself, say so, and show the blank outline if you have one.

Include these items:

- The material grade, or the strength you need if the grade is open
- The part weight
- Any surface treatment or coating
- Special notes, such as marking, traceability or packing

Also say what stage the drawing is at. A released drawing and a concept sketch need different handling. If the design may still change, tell the supplier now.

## Question 2: What volume do you need, and when?

Forging needs dies. Dies cost money before the first part ships. So the supplier needs to know how many parts you plan to buy.

Give three numbers:

- The sample or prototype quantity
- The expected annual volume
- The expected life volume, if you know it

Say whether you need a small first batch before series supply. Some buyers send every enquiry as a series quote, then find that they only wanted prototypes. Be clear, because the right tooling plan differs.

Also give your delivery timing as stages: sample, approval and series start. Do not ask for one single lead time. A single date hides the real steps. Ask the supplier to propose dates for each stage.

## Question 3: What quality level does the part need?

Decide the quality rules before you quote, not after.

Ask yourself:

- Which features are critical, and what tolerance do they need?
- Does the part need crack testing, and on how many parts?
- Which hardness or grain-size checks do you need?
- Which PPAP level will you require?
- Must each part trace back to its steel heat?

Safety-critical parts usually need more inspection than general parts. Write this in the RFQ. If it is not written down, the quote may not include it.

Also ask what the supplier measures during the process, not only at the end. Process control data on key dimensions tells you more than a final inspection list.

## Question 4: Which certificates and documents do you need?

Documents are part of the product. Missing paperwork can hold a shipment at your dock.

List what you require before you request a quote:

- The material certificate type, such as EN 10204 type 3.1, or 3.2 if a third party must witness
- The PPAP package and level
- Inspection reports and traceability records
- The quality system certificates the supplier must hold, such as IATF 16949

If you need a certificate level above the usual one, say so in the RFQ. It can change the cost and the timing.

## Question 5: What happens after you send the RFQ?

Ask the supplier how the process runs, and how fast it responds.

A clear sequence looks like this: drawing, feasibility review, quotation, then PPAP. Ask the supplier to confirm it. Ask also how long each step takes, and who your contact is.

Test the answer. A supplier that gives a firm response time for feasibility, with sensible limits, is easier to plan around than one that says "soon". Ask whether the supplier accepts prototype and low-volume orders, and whether you can visit the plant.

## How Varsha Forgings handles an RFQ

Varsha Forgings has made hot closed-die forgings since 1989 at its plant in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad). It forges carbon and alloy steels from 1 kg to 25 kg, on presses from 800 T to 2,500 T, and machines parts in-house. The stated tolerances are ±0.5 mm on forged features and ±0.02 mm on machined features.

The RFQ sequence is customer drawing, feasibility review, quotation, then PPAP. Varsha Forgings returns a feasibility response within 48 hours for a single drawing or part. A package of many parts takes longer, so do not expect 48 hours for it.

Drawings are accepted in 2D and 3D. Prototype and low-volume enquiries are welcome alongside series production. Material certificates are EN 10204 type 3.1, with type 3.2 on request. The plant is certified to IATF 16949 and supports PPAP at Levels 1 to 5. Customers are welcome to visit the plants after an RFQ, and virtual plant tours are available.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should an RFQ for forgings include?**
A 2D or 3D drawing, the material grade, the part weight, the annual volume, the quality and certificate requirements, and your delivery stages.

**How fast should a supplier respond?**
It depends on the request. Varsha Forgings gives a feasibility response within 48 hours for a single drawing or part.

**Can I send a prototype enquiry?**
Yes. Varsha Forgings accepts prototype and low-volume enquiries alongside series production.

**Which certificates can I request?**
EN 10204 type 3.1 is standard. Type 3.2 is available on request.

**Can I visit the plant?**
Customers are welcome to visit after an RFQ. Virtual plant tours are also available.
