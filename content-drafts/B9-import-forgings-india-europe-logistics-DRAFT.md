## The short answer

Sourcing forgings from India to Europe works well when you plan the whole chain, not only the piece price. That chain has five parts: the quote and approval, the certificates, the payment terms, the packing and shipping, and the timing.

Settle each part before the first purchase order. Most delays come from a missing document or an unclear agreement, not from the forging itself.

## Step 1: Approve the part before you plan shipping

Before any logistics, the part must be approved. The usual sequence is drawing, feasibility review, quotation, then PPAP. Samples come before series supply.

Ask the supplier to propose dates for each stage: tooling, sample, approval and series supply. Do not accept one single lead time. A single figure hides the steps, and it is the step you cannot see that slips.

Be careful with any supplier that quotes a firm lead time without seeing the drawing. A real date depends on the part, the tooling and the current workload.

## Step 2: Agree the certificates

For forged steel parts, the material certificate is the key document. The standard reference is EN 10204.

- **Type 3.1** is issued by the manufacturer. It states the steel grade and test results for the batch you receive.
- **Type 3.2** adds validation by an independent party or by the customer's representative. It costs more and takes more time, so ask for it only where you need it.

Also agree which other documents you need:

- The PPAP package and the level you require
- Inspection reports for the key dimensions
- Traceability records that link each part to its steel heat
- Quality system certificates, such as IATF 16949

State all of this in the purchase order. Do not rely on the supplier to guess.

## Step 3: Agree payment terms

Payment terms affect your cash and the supplier's risk. Two common terms for export orders are:

- **Letter of credit.** A bank guarantees payment once the supplier presents the agreed documents. It protects both sides, and the document list must match exactly.
- **Advance payment.** You pay all or part before dispatch. It is simple, but it puts the risk with you.

Check how your own bank handles each term, and what fees apply. Agree the delivery terms, known as Incoterms, in writing. They decide who pays for freight and who carries the risk at each point of the journey.

## Step 4: Plan packing and shipping

Steel forgings are heavy and can rust or get damaged in transit. Good packing protects the parts and satisfies customs.

Wooden packaging material that crosses borders must meet the international standard for wood treatment, called ISPM 15. Ask your supplier to confirm it, because untreated wood can be refused at the border.

Other points to settle:

- **Corrosion protection.** Ask how parts are protected on the way, and how long they will sit in a container.
- **Tracking.** Ask whether the container can be tracked.
- **Port.** Ask which Indian port the goods leave from, and which European port you prefer.
- **Freight forwarder.** Use a forwarder who knows your customs rules and can handle the paperwork.
- **Customs and duties.** Check the classification code and any duty or tax for your country with your forwarder or customs agent.

For samples and urgent needs, air freight is often better than sea freight. It costs more per kilogram but arrives sooner, and it suits a small number of parts.

## Step 5: Build a realistic timeline

Add the stages up, and add time for transit and customs.

1. Feasibility and quotation
2. Tooling and first samples
3. Sample shipping and your testing
4. Approval and PPAP
5. Series production and packing
6. Sea transit and customs clearance

Ask your supplier for dates on stages 1 to 5. Ask your forwarder for stage 6. Plan stock buffers for the first series orders, because early shipments often meet small delays. Visit the plant, or ask for a virtual tour, before the first big order.

## How Varsha Forgings supports European buyers

Varsha Forgings has made hot closed-die forgings since 1989 at its plant in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad). Export markets include Germany, Spain, Italy, Portugal and Austria. Forgings range from 1 kg to 25 kg in carbon and alloy steels, on presses from 800 T to 2,500 T, with in-house machining. The stated tolerances are ±0.5 mm on forged features and ±0.02 mm on machined features.

On documents, material certificates are EN 10204 type 3.1, with type 3.2 on request. The plant is certified to IATF 16949 and supports PPAP at Levels 1 to 5. Payment terms are letter of credit or advance payment. Raw material comes from tier-one steel mills, and each raw-material heat carries a batch code for traceability.

On shipping, Varsha Forgings uses ISPM-15 export crates and GPS-tracked containers, with access to the JNPT (Nhava Sheva) port. Air freight is available for samples and urgent customer needs. Virtual plant tours are available, and customers are welcome to visit after an RFQ.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which certificate should I ask for?**
EN 10204 type 3.1 covers most needs. Ask for type 3.2 only where independent validation is required. Varsha Forgings issues 3.1, and 3.2 on request.

**What payment terms does Varsha Forgings offer?**
Letter of credit or advance payment.

**How are forgings packed for export?**
In ISPM-15 export crates, shipped in GPS-tracked containers.

**Can I get samples by air?**
Yes. Air freight is available for samples and urgent customer needs.

**What should I send for a quote?**
A 2D or 3D drawing, the material grade, the part weight, the annual volume and your certificate needs. Varsha Forgings returns a feasibility response within 48 hours for a single drawing or part.
