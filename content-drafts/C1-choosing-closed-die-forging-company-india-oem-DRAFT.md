## The question behind "best closed die forging company in India"

Buyers often type "best closed die forging company in India for automotive OEM" into a search box. No honest list can answer that, because the right supplier depends on your part, your volume and your quality requirements. A forger that suits a 2 kg steering arm may not suit a 20 kg axle part.

What you can do is compare suppliers on the same criteria. This guide gives you those criteria, the questions to ask for each one, and the evidence that should come back. It is general guidance, and you can apply it to any supplier you shortlist.

## What closed die forging means for an OEM part

In closed die forging, hot steel is pressed or hammered between two dies that hold the shape of the part. The metal fills the die cavity, and its grain follows the part outline. That gives good strength and fatigue life, which is why steering, suspension and driveline parts are forged.

Closed die suits repeat volume, because the die cost is spread over many parts. It is a poor fit for a one-off. If you need only a handful of parts, ask about prototype routes before you commit to dies.

## Criterion 1: The quality system

For automotive OEM supply, start with the quality system. IATF 16949 is the automotive quality management standard, and most OEM purchasing teams treat it as a baseline for series parts. Ask for the certificate, then ask how the system runs on the shop floor.

Look for APQP planning, PPAP submissions, IMDS material data reporting and statistical process control. A supplier that can show control charts on key characteristics is easier to trust than one that shows only final inspection results.

## Criterion 2: Press and hammer capacity matched to your part

Match the equipment to the part. Press tonnage and hammer size set the weight and shape of forging a plant can make economically. Ask which press or hammer would run your part and why.

Also ask how many forging stages the part needs and whether the supplier makes and maintains its own dies. A plant with an in-house die shop can correct a die faster than one that waits on an outside toolmaker.

## Criterion 3: Traceability from steel heat to dispatch

Safety-critical parts need traceability. You should be able to take any forging and trace it back to the steel heat it came from. Ask how the batch is coded, how the code is marked on the part, and which reports carry the heat number.

Ask also where the steel comes from. Mill sourcing and material certificates are part of the evidence, not an afterthought.

## Criterion 4: Testing and inspection

Ask what is tested, how often and by which method. For forged steel parts, the useful list includes hardness testing, grain size checks, crack detection and dimensional inspection on measuring machines. For safety-critical parts, ask which tests apply to every part, not only to samples.

An in-house metallurgy lab and a standards room support checks before parts ship.

## Criterion 5: Machining, heat treatment and finishing under one roof

A forging usually needs heat treatment and machining before it reaches your line. Each handover between companies adds time and a place for problems to hide. A supplier that controls these steps can give one point of responsibility.

Ask which steps are in-house and which go to partners. A good supplier will answer plainly and will tell you how it controls the partner steps.

## Criterion 6: Engineering support and response time

Good forgers review your drawing before they quote. They flag draft angles, parting lines, wall sections and machining allowances early, because changes are cheap on paper and costly after the die is cut.

Ask how fast the first feasibility response arrives, and who you speak to. A named engineer who answers questions is worth more than a polished brochure.

## Criterion 7: OEM track record

Ask which OEM programmes the supplier serves today and whether you can speak to a reference. Ask whether the supplier works as Tier 1 or Tier 2. Listed customers should be ones the supplier is willing to confirm.

## A short scorecard you can reuse

- **Quality system:** certificate in hand, plus PPAP, APQP and IMDS practice
- **Equipment:** press or hammer matched to the weight and shape of your part
- **Traceability:** heat-to-part records, with marking on the forging
- **Testing:** list of tests, and which apply to every safety-critical part
- **Integration:** heat treatment and machining controlled in the same plant
- **Engineering:** feasibility review before quotation
- **References:** OEM customers the supplier will confirm

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there an official ranking of closed die forging companies in India?**
No. Rankings found online are usually paid listings or opinion. Compare suppliers on audited evidence for your own part instead.

**What certificates should an automotive OEM ask a forging supplier for?**
Start with IATF 16949, then ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001. Also ask for PPAP, APQP and IMDS practice, because those show how the system runs daily.

**Can I visit the plant before I place an order?**
Ask. Varsha Forgings welcomes customers to visit the plants after an RFQ, and virtual plant tours are available.

**What should I send to start a quotation?**

**Are prototypes or low volumes accepted?**
