## What PPAP Level 5 means

PPAP stands for Production Part Approval Process. It is the package of evidence that shows a supplier can make a part to your drawing, repeatedly, at the agreed rate. Customers ask for it before series supply starts.

There are five submission levels. Level 1 sends the warrant only. Level 2 adds samples and limited data. Level 3 adds samples and complete supporting data. Level 4 covers whatever the customer defines. Level 5 sends the warrant, product samples and complete supporting data, and the customer reviews all of it at the supplier's manufacturing location.

Level 5 is the most demanding level, because it puts the process itself on show, not only the paperwork.

## What the Level 5 evidence package contains

The exact list comes from your customer requirements, but a typical package for a forged and machined part includes:

- **Design records and drawing** with any engineering change history
- **Process flow diagram and control plan** that follow the part from steel to dispatch
- **Failure mode analysis** of the process, with the actions taken
- **Dimensional results** from a measured sample of parts
- **Material and test results**: chemistry, hardness, grain size and mechanical checks
- **Measurement system studies** for the gauges used
- **Capability data** showing that key characteristics stay in control
- **Sample parts** and a signed part submission warrant

The point is that every item should trace to real production data, not to a template.

## Why a Level 5 review is on site

At Level 5, the customer team walks the line. They compare the control plan with what operators actually do, look at how parts are marked and stored, and check that gauges are calibrated and in use.

For a forging plant, that means the die shop, the presses or hammers, the heat treatment area, the testing lab and the inspection room. A plant that works to its control plan every day passes this walk easily. A plant that prepares only for the visit does not.

## The plant capability behind the paperwork

A supplier can only submit good evidence if the plant can generate it. Ask to see these capabilities:

**Process planning and simulation.** Forging simulation shows metal flow before dies are cut, which reduces trial and error.

**Die control.** In-house die making and maintenance keep dimensions stable across a run.

**Process control.** Statistical process control on key characteristics gives the capability data a package needs.

**Material verification.** A spectrometer and metallurgy lab support incoming material verification.

**Measurement.** Coordinate measuring machines and profile instruments produce dimensional results you can trust.

**Traceability.** Heat-to-part records tie every sample to a steel heat.

## Traceability in the package

A good PPAP package lets you pick up any sample part and trace it. Look for a batch code for each raw-material heat, a mark on the forging or die, and reports that carry the heat number and traceability code.

This also protects you later. If a problem appears in the field, traceable records let the supplier and customer narrow the affected batch quickly.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between PPAP Level 3 and Level 5?**
Both send the warrant, samples and complete supporting data. At Level 5 the customer reviews the evidence at the supplier's manufacturing location.

**Does an IATF 16949 certificate replace PPAP?**
No. The certificate covers the quality system. PPAP shows that a specific part can be made to specification, repeatedly.

**Can a forging supplier submit PPAP for a prototype part?**
Often at a lower level, while the process is still being proven. Agree the level with your supplier early.

**Which PPAP levels does Varsha Forgings support?**
Levels 1 to 5.

**What should I send for a PPAP-ready quotation?**
A 2D or 3D drawing, the material grade, the part weight, annual volume and the required PPAP level.
