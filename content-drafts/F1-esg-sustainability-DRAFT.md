### H2: Management Systems That Cover Environment and People

Two certified management systems apply directly to environmental and safety performance. ISO 14001:2015 is the international standard for an environmental management system. ISO 45001:2018 is the standard for occupational health and safety. We hold both, alongside ISO 9001:2015 and IATF 16949 for quality.

We also hold CQI-9. CQI-9 is the heat treat system assessment used in the automotive supply chain, and heat treatment is one of the most energy-intensive steps in making a forged component. It is a process-control standard, not an environmental one, but it governs the furnaces where much of the energy goes.

These certificates describe a management system: documented responsibilities, objectives, audits and corrective action. They do not by themselves state an emissions figure, and we do not present them as if they did.

### H2: Where a Forging Plant Uses Energy

General engineering background, not a claim about any one supplier. A forging plant uses most of its energy in two places. The first is heating the steel billet to forging temperature. The second is heat treatment of the forged part, such as hardening, tempering and normalizing.

At Varsha, billets are heated by induction to between 1,100 and 1,250 degrees C before they go to the presses and hammers. Heat treatment follows in continuous, batch and tempering furnaces, with hardness testing and metallurgical checks afterwards. Forging is hot closed-die only, shaped close to the final form so that less material has to be removed by machining. That is a property of the process in general. We do not publish a material-yield or scrap figure.

### H2: For European Buyers Assessing CBAM Exposure

The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) puts carbon-cost reporting and certificate obligations on importers of covered goods above a single 50-tonne annual mass threshold (authorised-declarant status and certificate surrender apply above it). Covered goods include iron and steel at sector level. Its definitive period began on 1 January 2026. Whether a particular forged component falls inside CBAM depends on its customs classification and on the rules in force on the import date, which continue to be refined. The European Commission has proposed extending CBAM to roughly 180 downstream steel and aluminium-intensive products from 1 January 2028; this is a proposal, not law, and has not yet been adopted by Parliament. Confirm it with your customs adviser and the current regulation text. This is general background and not legal advice.

What a buyer can reasonably ask of a supplier in this position:

- Which certified environmental and safety management systems it holds, and the scope of each.
- Who owns its energy assets, and what it can document about them.
- What material traceability exists back to the steel heat, because embedded-emissions questions start with the steel.
- What data it can provide for the parts you buy.

We answer the first three on this page and in our quality documentation. Our raw material comes from tier-one steel mills, with EN 10204 type 3.1 material certificates, and 3.2 on request. Every batch carries a code per raw-material heat, carried on our inspection reports. Raise any embedded-emissions data request at enquiry so that it is part of the quotation discussion and not a surprise at the first shipment.
