### H2: About These Case Studies

Each case below follows the same order: problem, constraint, solution and outcome. Customers are not named, and we publish no performance figures. The descriptions say what the engineering challenge was and how our process addressed it. Varsha Forgings Private Limited has made hot closed-die forgings since 1989, in a range from 1 kg to 25 kg.

### H2: Case 1. A Steering Lever for a European Commercial-Vehicle OEM

**Problem.** A European commercial-vehicle OEM needed a forged steering lever for a heavy-duty vehicle. The part is safety-critical: a failure in service is not acceptable, so the buyer's questions concerned fatigue behaviour, grain flow and proof that every part had been checked.

**Constraint.** The geometry had a thick hub and a slender arm, which is hard to fill in a closed die without laps or underfill. The part also had to meet the buyer's PPAP submission and carry full traceability to the steel heat.

**Solution.** Our engineers simulated the metal flow in SimuFact Forming before cutting the die, adjusting the billet shape and preform until the die filled cleanly. Billets were heated by induction between 1,100 and 1,250 degrees C and forged hot in a closed die cut in H13 tool steel. After heat treatment, we checked hardness and grain size, and ran 100% magnetic particle inspection on the part because it is safety-critical. Each batch carried a code per raw-material heat, and the material certificate was EN 10204 type 3.1.

**Outcome.** The part moved through the buyer's PPAP process on documented evidence: simulation, process-control data at a capability index of 1.67 or better, inspection records and traceability. The buyer could trace any part back to its steel heat.

### H2: Case 2. A Driveline Gear Blank for a North American Off-Highway Manufacturer

**Problem.** A North American off-highway equipment manufacturer needed forged gear blanks in a case-hardening steel. The gear cutter downstream depended on a consistent blank: any variation in hardness or grain size would show up as variation in the finished gear.

**Constraint.** The blank had to hold a tight machined tolerance on the bore and faces, with a forged condition predictable enough for the buyer's own gear process. Machining tolerance was plus or minus 0.02 mm on machined features, against plus or minus 0.5 mm on forged features.

**Solution.** We forged the blank in 20MnCr5 on a press from our 800 to 2,500 T range, then normalized it so the structure was uniform for machining. We machined faces and bore in-house, with hobbing and grinding in-house where the drawing needed them. Hardness was tested by Brinell and Rockwell and grain size to ASTM E112, and dimensions were checked on a coordinate measuring machine and a profile projector.

**Outcome.** The buyer received blanks with a consistent, documented forged and normalized condition, with measurement records supporting the machined dimensions, ready for their downstream gear process.

### H2: Case 3. A Prototype Run for a European Tier 1 Supplier

**Problem.** A European Tier 1 supplier had a new design and needed a small number of forged samples before committing to series tooling. They wanted to see forged metal before committing to the full tooling programme.

**Constraint.** Few parts, one drawing, and a fixed internal review date. The buyer also wanted to know early whether the part was forgeable at all.

**Solution.** We accept prototype and low-volume enquiries alongside series production. For a single drawing we return a feasibility response within 48 hours. Our feasibility review covered parting line, draft, fill and likely tolerance. For the small quantity we used a friction drop hammer from our 1 to 3 MT range, with a die cut using Dell Cam 3D programming. The drawing was accepted in 2D and 3D.

**Outcome.** The buyer saw a feasibility verdict early, got forged samples to test against the design, and used the result to decide on series tooling. The same RFQ sequence, drawing, feasibility, quotation, PPAP, then carried the part into series.
