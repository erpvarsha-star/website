### Body copy

**H1: How to Source Closed-Die Forgings from India: A Buyer's Guide**

A buyer sources closed-die forgings from India by sending a drawing to a certified supplier for a feasibility review, receiving a quotation, and running the part through PPAP before series production begins. For a single drawing or part, an established Indian forging supplier should return a feasibility response within 48 hours. The buyer should confirm the supplier's certifications (IATF 16949, ISO 9001:2015, PPAP capability at the required level), material certification standard (EN 10204 3.1 or 3.2), weight and tolerance range, and export logistics (freight method, crating, payment terms) before committing volume. This guide walks through each step an OEM or Tier-1 sourcing engineer in Germany, the US, Japan or elsewhere actually takes to qualify and order from a closed-die forging supplier in India.

**H2: The RFQ sequence — from drawing to PPAP**

Sourcing a forged part from India follows a fixed sequence, and knowing it in advance saves a buyer several rounds of back-and-forth. The sequence is: customer drawing, feasibility review, quotation, and PPAP (Production Part Approval Process). At Varsha Forgings, a feasibility response for a single drawing or part is returned within 48 hours; a multi-part package naturally takes longer, since each part needs its own feasibility check. Prototype and low-volume enquiries are accepted alongside series production, so a buyer evaluating a new supplier does not need to commit to full annual volume just to get a first part qualified. Customers are welcome to visit the plants once an RFQ is open, which lets a sourcing engineer see the press line, the heat-treatment shop and the quality lab before placing a production order — useful when qualifying an overseas supplier for the first time.

**H2: What to send with your RFQ**

The faster and more complete the first submission, the faster the feasibility response comes back. A complete RFQ package includes:

- **The part drawing**, accepted in both 2D and 3D formats.
- **Material grade.** Varsha Forgings works in carbon and alloy steels — grades such as EN8, EN9, EN19, EN24, EN31, 20MnCr5, 16MnCr5, 42CrMo4, 20MoCr4E, SAE1541, SAE8620, AISI 4140, AISI 1045, ASTM A105, LF2, F11 and F22 — sourced from tier-one steel mills. Stainless steel is not forged here, so a buyer with a stainless requirement should confirm that upfront.
- **Finished part weight.** The practical forging range is 1 kg to 25 kg; a part well outside that range should be flagged early so the supplier can say so in the feasibility review rather than at quotation stage.
- **Annual volume**, even as an estimate, since it affects tooling cost and press selection.
- **The PPAP level required** for the part, since India-based Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers submit PPAP at different levels depending on the customer's risk classification for that component.

As general background: alloying elements change what a forged steel grade can do in service. Chromium and molybdenum (as in 42CrMo4 or AISI 4140) raise hardenability and strength after quench-and-temper, which is why those grades suit shafts, arms and links under cyclic load; manganese-chromium case-hardening grades (20MnCr5, 16MnCr5, SAE8620) are chosen where a hard wear surface is needed over a tough core. Naming the right grade on the drawing, rather than leaving it to the supplier to guess, shortens the feasibility step.

**H2: Certifications and quality proof points to check**

Before shortlisting an Indian forging supplier, a buyer should ask for proof of: IATF 16949 certification (the automotive quality management standard), ISO 9001:2015, ISO 45001:2018 (occupational health and safety) and ISO 14001:2015 (environmental management), PPAP submission capability at Levels 1 through 5, APQP, and IMDS reporting for material declarations. Varsha Forgings holds all of the above, plus RDSO approval for Indian Railways applications.

On the shop floor, the proof points worth asking about are statistical process control on key characteristics (held at Cpk of 1.67 or better here), the tolerance band actually achieved — ±0.5 mm on the forged dimension and ±0.02 mm on machined features — and whether safety-critical parts get 100% magnetic particle inspection, not sampling. Ask too how the supplier traces a finished part back to its raw material: each raw-material heat should carry its own batch code, engraved on the die or marked on the part surface, with the heat number carried through setup records, in-process inspection, the Material Test Report and the Part/Dimensional Inspection Report.

**H2: Freight, crating, lead times and payment terms**

Once a part is approved, the commercial terms that matter most for an overseas buyer are the ones that affect landed cost and risk, not just the piece price:

- **Material certificates:** issued to EN 10204 type 3.1 as standard, with type 3.2 available on request for parts that need third-party witnessed testing.
- **Payment terms:** Letter of Credit or advance payment.
- **Export packing:** ISPM-15 compliant wooden crates (required for any wood packaging entering most export markets) and GPS-tracked containers for shipment visibility in transit.
- **Port access:** shipments move through JNPT (Nhava Sheva), India's principal container port for west-coast exports.
- **Samples and urgent needs:** air freight is available, so a first-sample set does not have to wait for a full container.
- **Plant access without travel:** virtual plant tours are available for buyers who want to see the operation before an in-person visit is practical.

A buyer should ask any Indian forging supplier to confirm these points in writing at quotation stage, since they affect total landed cost and delivery risk as much as the unit price does.
