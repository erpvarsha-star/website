**H1: Forged Components for Electric Vehicles**

**H2: Which EV components does Varsha Forgings make?**

Our EV work draws on the same component families we already forge and machine for conventional vehicles, where the load cases are similar or harsher:

- **Suspension parts:** swing arms (RH and LH), eye ends, spherical ball pins, connectors, housing cups and monoshock components.
- **Steering parts:** idler arms, sector shafts, drop arms, worms, end covers and spring saddles.
- **Driveline and chassis parts:** upper control arms, yokes, shaft joints, front wheel hubs, pinions, planet gears, helical gears and wheel carriers.

Each part is produced to the customer's drawing and material specification, then machined and inspected before dispatch.

**H2: Why use forgings in an electric vehicle?**

Electric vehicles carry heavy battery packs low in the chassis and deliver high torque from standstill. That places sustained and peak loads on suspension, steering and driveline parts. Closed-die forging aligns the metal's grain flow with the shape of the part, which gives better fatigue strength and impact toughness than casting or machining from bar. For safety-critical EV parts, that margin matters.

Forging also produces near-net-shape parts. Less material is machined away, which lowers the weight and cost of each part and reduces the machining time an EV programme has to pay for at volume.

**H2: How are EV parts made and checked?**

EV components follow our standard process route: material inspection, CNC circular saw cutting, billet heating, forging on presses or hammers, inspection, heat treatment, hardness testing, shot blasting, grinding and deburring, crack testing, machining, painting and final inspection.

Our forging line runs a 2,500 T Erie press, two 1,300 T press lines and an 800 T screw press, alongside 3 MT, 2 MT, 1.5 MT and 1 MT hammers. Heat treatment runs on a continuous tube furnace rated at 1 tonne per hour, a batch-type furnace, an electrical tempering furnace and an isothermal annealing furnace. Chemistry is verified on a Baird vacuum emission spectrometer, microstructure under a microscope with image analyser, and surface cracks with Magnaflux magnetic particle inspection.

**H2: Can every EV part be traced back to its raw material?**

Yes. Every raw-material heat receives its own batch code (A, B, C through Z). The code is engraved on the forging die for ball studs and ball pins, or marked on the part surface for sockets and housings. Setup and in-process inspection reports carry the raw-material heat number, and the final Material Test Report and Pre-Dispatch Inspection Report record the heat number, traceability code and invoice number. If an EV programme needs to trace a field part, it can be followed back to the heat it came from.

Varsha Forgings holds IATF 16949, ISO 9001:2015, ISO 45001:2018 and ISO 14001:2015 certification, with PPAP Level 3 submission capability for new part approvals.

Closed-Die Forging: Process, Presses & Capabilities | Varsha Forgings

### Body copy

**H1: Closed-Die Forging at Varsha Forgings**

Closed-die forging shapes a heated steel billet between two dies under high pressure, so the metal fills a precise cavity and takes the shape of the part. Varsha Forgings has run closed-die forging at its Aurangabad plant since 1989, producing alloy steel forgings and machined components for commercial vehicle, agricultural, industrial and electric vehicle OEMs.

**H2: What is closed-die forging?**

In closed-die forging, also called impression-die forging, the dies enclose the workpiece. As the press or hammer strikes, the hot metal flows into every part of the die cavity. The result is a near-net-shape part with consistent dimensions from one piece to the next and a grain flow that follows the part's contours. That continuous grain flow is what gives forged parts their strength and fatigue resistance.

**H2: How does closed-die forging compare with open-die forging and casting?**

- **Open-die forging** works the metal between flat or simple dies that do not enclose it. It suits large, simple shapes and short runs, but cannot match the dimensional repeatability or detail of closed-die parts.
- **Casting** pours molten metal into a mould. It handles complex shapes, but the metal has no directional grain flow and can contain porosity, so cast parts are generally weaker under fatigue and impact loads.
- **Closed-die forging** combines repeatable, detailed shapes with the strength of worked metal, which is why it is used for steering, suspension and driveline parts where failure is not acceptable.

**H2: What equipment does Varsha Forgings use?**

- **Presses:** a 2,500 T Erie press, two 1,300 T press lines and an 800 T screw press.
- **Hammers:** 3 MT, 2 MT, 1.5 MT and 1 MT.
- **Die shop:** five VMCs, an Etchon laser machine, radial drilling and Dell Cam 3D programming software, so dies are designed and made in-house.
- **Material cutting:** three CNC circular saws, two shearing machines and three band saws.

**H2: What happens after a part is forged?**

Every forging moves through a fixed sequence: inspection, heat treatment, hardness testing, shot blasting, grinding and deburring, crack testing, machining, painting and final inspection.

Heat treatment runs on a continuous tube furnace at 1 tonne per hour, a batch-type furnace, an electrical tempering furnace and an isothermal annealing furnace. The metallurgy lab checks chemistry on a Baird vacuum emission spectrometer and microstructure on a microscope with image analyser, alongside spark testing, polishing and hardness testing. Surface cracks are found with Magnaflux magnetic particle inspection at 400 A and 200 A. Dimensions are verified in the standards room on a Faro Arm CMM, a CMM, a Mitutoyo profile projector and a Zeiss contour tester.

**H2: How is each forging traced?**

Each raw-material heat gets a batch code. The code is engraved on the die or marked on the part, carried on every setup and in-process report, and recorded with the invoice number on the final Material Test Report and Pre-Dispatch Inspection Report.

### FAQ

**What is closed-die forging?**
A process that shapes heated metal between two dies that enclose the part, producing near-net-shape components with grain flow that follows the part's contours.

**What press capacity does Varsha Forgings have?**
A 2,500 T Erie press, two 1,300 T press lines, an 800 T screw press, and hammers from 1 MT to 3 MT.

**Is closed-die forging stronger than casting?**
Generally yes. Forged parts have directional grain flow and no casting porosity, which gives better fatigue and impact strength.
