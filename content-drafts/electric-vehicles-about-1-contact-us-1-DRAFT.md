**H2: Why choose forged components for load-bearing parts?**

Suspension and steering parts see sustained and peak loads over their life. As a general engineering principle, closed-die forging aligns the metal's grain flow with the shape of the part, which gives better fatigue strength and impact toughness than casting or machining from bar. For safety-critical parts, that margin matters.

In practice a forged part starts as a billet, cut on CNC circular saws, shearing machines or band saws, then heated and forged on a press or hammer before heat treatment, hardness testing, shot blasting, grinding and deburring, crack testing and machining.

Closed-Die Forging: Process, Presses & Capabilities | Varsha Forgings

### Body copy

**H1: Closed-Die Forging at Varsha Forgings**

Closed-die forging shapes a heated steel billet between two dies under high pressure, so the metal fills a precise cavity and takes the shape of the part. Varsha Forgings has run closed-die forging at its Aurangabad plant since 1989, producing alloy steel forgings of 1 kg to 25 kg and machined components for OEMs, including products developed for electric vehicles.

**H2: What is closed-die forging?**

In closed-die forging, also called impression-die forging, the dies enclose the workpiece. As the press or hammer strikes, the hot metal flows into every part of the die cavity. The result is a near-net-shape part with consistent dimensions from one piece to the next and a grain flow that follows the part's contours. That continuous grain flow is what gives forged parts their strength and fatigue resistance.

**H2: How does closed-die forging compare with casting?**

- **Casting** pours molten metal into a mould. It handles complex shapes, but the metal has no directional grain flow and can contain porosity, so cast parts are generally weaker under fatigue and impact loads.
- **Closed-die forging** combines repeatable, detailed shapes with the strength of worked metal, which is why it is used for steering and suspension parts where failure is not acceptable.

**H2: What equipment does Varsha Forgings use?**

- **Presses:** from 800 T to 2,500 T: a 2,500 T Erie press, two 1,300 T press lines, a recently added 1,000 T press and an 800 T screw press.
- **Billet heating:** induction heating at 1,100 to 1,250 °C.
- **Hammers:** 3 MT, 2 MT, 1.5 MT and 1 MT.
- **Die shop:** VMC machining, an Etchon laser machine, radial drilling and Dell Cam 3D programming software, with dies in H13 and DIN 1.2714 (DB6) steel, so tooling is handled in-house.
- **Material cutting:** CNC circular saw cutting, shearing and band saws.

**H2: What happens after a part is forged?**

Every forging moves through a fixed sequence: inspection, heat treatment, hardness testing, shot blasting, grinding and deburring, crack testing, machining, painting and final inspection.

Heat treatment runs on a continuous tube furnace at 1 tonne per hour, a batch-type furnace, an electrical tempering furnace and an isothermal annealing furnace. The metallurgy lab checks chemistry on a Baird vacuum emission spectrometer and microstructure on a microscope with image analyser, alongside spark testing, polishing and hardness testing. Surface cracks are found with Magnaflux magnetic particle inspection at 400 A and 200 A. Dimensions are verified in the standards room on a Faro Arm CMM, a CMM, a Mitutoyo profile projector and a Zeiss contour tester. Forging tolerance is ±0.5 mm and machining tolerance ±0.02 mm. Surface and heat treatments offered include vacuum carburizing, induction hardening, hot-dip galvanizing and double quench and temper.

**H2: How is each forging traced?**

Each raw-material heat gets a batch code. The code is engraved on the die or marked on the part, carried on every setup and in-process report, and recorded with the invoice number on the final MTR and PDIR.

### FAQ

**What is closed-die forging?**
A process that shapes heated metal between two dies that enclose the part, producing near-net-shape components with grain flow that follows the part's contours.

**What press capacity does Varsha Forgings have?**
Presses from 800 T to 2,500 T: a 2,500 T Erie press, two 1,300 T press lines, a 1,000 T press and an 800 T screw press, plus hammers from 1 MT to 3 MT.

**Is closed-die forging stronger than casting?**
Generally yes. Forged parts have directional grain flow and no casting porosity, which gives better fatigue and impact strength.
