### Body copy

**H1: Alloy Steel Forgings from Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad), India**

Varsha Forgings makes alloy steel and carbon steel forgings by hot closed-die forging at its Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad) plant, where the Forging Division was established in 1989. Forged components range from 1 kg to 25 kg, and annual capacity is over 12,000 metric tons and over 3 million assorted pieces. Parts are heat treated, tested, machined and, where required, assembled under one IATF 16949 certified supplier, for automotive, commercial vehicle, railway, agriculture, oil and gas, mining, construction, infrastructure and defense customers.

**H2: Which alloy and carbon steel grades does Varsha Forgings forge?**

The grades currently offered are EN8, EN9, EN19, EN24, EN31, 20MnCr5, 16MnCr5, 42CrMo4, 20MoCr4E, SAE1541, SAE8620, AISI 4140, AISI 1045, ASTM A105, LF2, F11 and F22. These are carbon and alloy steels. Varsha Forgings does not forge stainless steel. Raw material is sourced from tier-one steel mills, and material certificates are issued to EN 10204 type 3.1, with type 3.2 available on request.

As general engineering background, alloying elements change what a steel can do. Chromium and molybdenum raise hardenability and strength after quenching and tempering, which is why grades such as 42CrMo4, EN19 and AISI 4140 are common for shafts, arms and links under cyclic loads. Manganese-chromium case-hardening grades such as 20MnCr5, 16MnCr5 and SAE8620 are chosen where a hard wear surface is needed over a tough core.

**H2: How are alloy steel forgings made at Varsha Forgings?**

Forging is hot closed-die only. Billets are cut on CNC circular saws, shearing machines or band saws, heated by induction to between 1,100 and 1,250 °C, and forged on presses or hammers. The Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad) plant runs presses from 800 T to 2,500 T: a 2,500 T Erie press, two 1,300 T press lines, a recently added 1,000 T press and an 800 T screw press. Friction drop hammers of 3 MT, 2 MT, 1.5 MT and 1 MT handle parts that suit hammer forging.

Dies are made in-house in H13 and DIN 1.2714 (DB6) die steels. Die design and programming use Creo 3.0, BricsCAD and Dell Cam 3D programming software, and SimuFact Forming 15.0 simulates metal flow before dies are cut. The die shop adds VMC machining, an Etchon laser machine and radial drilling.

The full process flow is material inspection, cutting, billet heating, forging, inspection, heat treatment, hardness testing, shot blasting, grinding and deburring, crack testing, machining, painting and final inspection.

**H2: What heat treatment is available for alloy steel forgings?**

Heat treatment is done in-house on a continuous tube furnace rated at 1 tonne per hour, a batch-type furnace, an electrical tempering furnace and an isothermal annealing furnace. Treatments offered include normalizing, stress relieving, double quench and temper, vacuum carburizing and induction hardening, with hot-dip galvanizing available as a surface treatment. In general terms, normalizing refines grain structure and relieves forging stress, quenching and tempering sets the strength and toughness balance, and carburizing or induction hardening hardens the surface of parts that wear.

**H2: How are alloy steel forgings tested and held to tolerance?**

Forging tolerance is ±0.5 mm and machining tolerance is ±0.02 mm. The metallurgy lab runs a Baird vacuum emission spectrometer for chemistry, a microscope with image analyser, spark testing, polishing and hardness testing, covering Brinell, Rockwell and micro-Vickers methods, with ASTM E112 grain size checks. Ultrasonic testing is used, and magnetic particle inspection on Magnaflux 400 A and 200 A units is done on 100% of safety-critical parts. Dimensions are verified in the standards room on a Faro Arm CMM, a CMM, a Mitutoyo profile projector and a Zeiss contour tester. Key control characteristics are held under SPC at Cpk of 1.67 or better.

**H2: Can an alloy steel forging be machined and supplied finished?**

Yes. In-house machining covers facing and centering, CNC and VMC machining, special-purpose machines, drilling, gear hobbing and grinding. A dedicated base of machining vendors extends this capacity with milling, broaching, turning, tapping and thread rolling; broaching is a vendor process. Finished parts include crankshafts, connecting rods, steering knuckles, camshafts, axle shafts, flanges and valve bodies, tow hooks and bearing bushes.

**H2: Is every forging traceable to its heat?**

Each raw-material heat receives a batch code (A, B, C through Z). The code is engraved on the forging die for ball studs and ball pins, or marked on the surface of sockets and housings. Setup and in-process reports carry the raw-material heat number, and the final MTR and PDIR carry the heat number, traceability code and invoice number.

**H2: How do I start an enquiry?**

Send your drawing, in 2D or 3D, with material grade and annual volume. The sequence is customer drawing, feasibility review, quotation and PPAP, which is available at Levels 1 to 5. A feasibility response is given within 48 hours for a single drawing or part. Prototype and low-volume enquiries are accepted alongside series production. Export customers are served in Germany, Spain, Italy, Portugal, Austria, the United States, Asia-Pacific and the Middle East & Africa.

### Body copy

**H1: Forged Steering and Suspension Components**

Steering and suspension parts carry the loads that keep a vehicle controllable, so they are safety-critical forgings. Varsha Forgings forges these parts at Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad), machines them, and builds assemblies at its Pune plant, which has operated since 2007 as the Maxility suspension and machining division. The company is IATF 16949 certified and supplies OEMs as a Tier 1 and Tier 2 supplier, including Daimler, Ashok Leyland, MAN Trucks India, Mahindra Trucks & Buses, Paccar, Scania and Tata Daewoo.

**H2: Which steering components does Varsha Forgings make?**

The steering range includes idler arms, sector shafts, end covers, spring saddles, worms and drop arms, together with ball joints, drag links, tie rods, steering levers and steering knuckles. In a steering system these parts transfer driver input to the wheels and absorb road shock, so strength and fatigue life matter more than weight savings alone.

**H2: Which suspension components and assemblies are available?**

Suspension parts include eye ends, spherical ball pins, right-hand and left-hand swing arms, connectors, housing cups and monoshock components, along with anti-roll bar linkages, axle mounts and swing-arm mounts. For commercial vehicles, Varsha Forgings makes torque rods, radius rods and V-links. As general background, a torque rod restrains axle rotation under drive and brake torque, a radius rod holds the axle in position along the vehicle, and a V-link controls the axle both sideways and lengthwise through one three-point part.

Control-rod assemblies, both adjustable and crimped, are also made by Varsha Forgings. Rubber-bonded parts such as engine mounts, vibration isolators, NVH bushes and bonded housings are supplied through a dedicated rubber-bonding partner, under Varsha Forgings' quality control.

**H2: Why are steering and suspension parts forged?**

As general engineering principle, closed-die forging aligns the grain flow of the steel with the contour of the part. Parts that see repeated and peak loads benefit from that alignment because it improves resistance to fatigue cracking and impact compared with a casting, which has no directional grain. For safety-critical parts that margin is the reason to forge.

Varsha Forgings uses hot closed-die forging only, on presses from 800 T to 2,500 T and hammers of 1 to 3 MT, for components of 1 kg to 25 kg. Typical grades include EN19, EN24, 42CrMo4, 20MnCr5 and SAE1541. Forging tolerance is ±0.5 mm and machining tolerance is ±0.02 mm.

**H2: How are steering and suspension parts checked?**

Heat treatment is done in-house, followed by hardness testing, shot blasting, grinding and deburring, and crack testing. Magnetic particle inspection is done on 100% of safety-critical parts. Dimensions are verified on a Faro Arm CMM, a CMM, a Mitutoyo profile projector and a Zeiss contour tester. Key control characteristics are held under SPC at Cpk of 1.67 or better. Every raw-material heat has a batch code carried through setup and in-process reports, and the final MTR and PDIR record the heat number, traceability code and invoice number.

**H2: How are assemblies built and delivered?**

At Pune, assembly uses hydraulic presses of 5 to 50 T and automated circlip applicators. Bolts are tightened to torque classes 8.8, 10.9 and 12.9, with pressure-decay leak testing and a 100% torque audit. Supply from Pune follows JIT and Kanban to the Chakan, Talegaon and Bhosari OEM belt, and export shipments use ISPM-15 crates and GPS-tracked containers through JNPT (Nhava Sheva).

**H2: Does this range include electric vehicles?**
